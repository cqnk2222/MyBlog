This is my princeple for the problem:

**Goal — Minimize Reader Cost**
Minimize the time a reader spends to locate, understand, and verify each claim. 

**1. Figure-first**
Key arguments are carried by figures, and each caption states the takeaway, not just what is plotted. A reader who sees only the figures and captions should still get the main results.

**2. Question → Answer → Evidence**
Each section or paragraph first states the question it answers, then gives the answer, then the supporting evidence. Readers can stop once they are convinced.


**3. Evidence Over Narrative (Calibrated Claims)**
Every claim is backed by data, proof, or citation, and its wording is no stronger than its evidence. Narrative only connects evidence; it never substitutes for it.


**4. Mathematical Clarity & Consistent Terms**
Define every symbol and term before use, keep notation consistent throughout, and state assumptions explicitly. Use formalism only where it buys precision.


**5. Scientific Visualization Loop**
Plot, check whether the figure answers its intended question at a glance, then revise the data selection, encoding, or comparison and repeat. Figures are tools for finding problems, not just displaying results.


**6. Natural, Precise Writing**
Use concrete verbs and specific numbers, and cut filler and empty transitions. Avoid AI-style patterns: stacked adjectives, reflexive triplets, a summary sentence closing every paragraph, and excessive hedging.

**7. Honest Limitations**
State where the method applies, how it behaves when assumptions break, and where it fails. Stating limits proactively is cheaper than having reviewers find them.


总体而言：**不能让 AI 发散回复**，否则输出会远远超出人的工作记忆容量。

在以下方面进行了限制：
- **Figure**
- **Claim**
- **Symbol**
- **Writing Style**

2. **反问自己**。
